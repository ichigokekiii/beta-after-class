# AfterClass Pitch Deck — Partner Review & Rebuild Brief

**Source:** `AfterClass_Pitch_Deck.pptx` (12 slides)  
**Framework:** YC / Sequoia narrative structure, adapted for **café & campus partnership** (not investor fundraising)  
**Context:** Live product in Metro Manila with real traction data available  
**Goal of this doc:** Score the current deck, map gaps, and specify exactly what to add so partners say yes.

---

## Executive Verdict

The deck already does several hard things well: clear one-liner, meet-first product logic, two-sided value framing, and a concrete founding-partner ask. For café partners, that is the right spine.

What holds it back is **proof density**. Partners do not buy vision the way investors do — they buy **predictable foot traffic, low risk, and a clear “what happens in my shop next week.”** Right now the strongest claims (“guaranteed student foot traffic,” “real matches and meetups already happening”) are qualitative. You have real data; the next deck must put those numbers on the page and make the café the hero of the conversion story.

**Bottom line:** Keep the 12-slide partnership arc. Rebuild traction, offer, and proof slides around real metrics. Add Why Now + Team. Soften unprovable guarantees. Show the product and Spot UX.

---

## Audience Shift: Investor Deck vs Partner Deck

| Investor cares about | Café partner cares about |
|----------------------|--------------------------|
| TAM / SAM / SOM | How many students near *my* location |
| MoM growth / retention | Visits / week they can expect |
| LTV / CAC | Cost, risk, and payback for listing |
| Moat vs Tinder | Why students choose *my* café over the one next door |
| Raise + runway | Founding terms, exclusivity, what “featured” means |
| Team as builders | Team as operators who will actually send people |

Use YC structure for **clarity and sequencing**. Rewrite every slide’s “so what?” for a café owner / GM / marketing lead in NCR.

## Slide-by-Slide Analysis

### Slide 1 — Title / Hook
**Current:** Product & Partnership Deck · “Where college connections happen in real life.” · 1,000+ active students · Live in Metro Manila

**What’s working**
- Correct audience label (“PRODUCT & PARTNERSHIP DECK”)
- Strong category line: school-verified → real-world coffee dates

**Gaps**

**Include next**

### Slide 2 — Problem (“The Disconnect”)
**Current:** Trust low, chats go nowhere, campuses siloed · 79% Gen Z disconnected · No Trust / No Momentum

**What’s working**
- Visceral framing; three clean pain points
- Ends with a local punchline (Metro Manila students)

**Gaps**
- 79% Gen Z is global / soft — partners discount it
- No café-side problem (empty weekday afternoons, hard to reach verified students, promo ROI unclear)
- No local quantification (chat-to-meet conversion, safety concerns, multi-campus siloing in NCR)

**Include next (use real data where you have it)**
- Student side (pick 1–2 real numbers):
  - % of matches that never meet (or baseline industry + your observed lift)
  - Time from match → meetup vs chat apps
  - Schools / districts covered and how siloed dating feels today
- Café side (mandatory for partner deck):
  - “Cafés near campuses compete for the same students but lack a **verified, intent-rich** channel.”
  - Weekday daytime whitespace: students meet after class; that is your Spot window
- Replace or demote the 79% stat unless you can localize it to PH / Manila students

**YC template rewrite**
> College students in Metro Manila want to meet peers from other campuses, but open dating apps feel unsafe and chat-heavy. Matches die in DMs. Cafés near campuses want student traffic but have no way to capture people who already decided to meet today.

### Slide 7 — Market Opportunity
**Current:** 3.5M PH college students · 8.4% coffee-shop CAGR · NCR first · expand Cebu/Davao/Iloilo

**What’s working**
- Geographic wedge is smart
- Coffee CAGR nods at partner industry

**Gaps**
- Classic investor TAM slide — weak for a café owner in QC
- “1% of TAM” risk even if unspoken: big national numbers don’t sell a founding Spot
- No bottom-up partner math

**Include next (bottom-up, YC-preferred)**
1. **Local density:** students within X km of campus clusters you serve  
2. **Meetup frequency:** expected AfterClass meetups / week in that cluster  
3. **Partner share:** if you have 10–20 Spots, what volume each Spot might see  
4. **Spend assumption:** conservative ticket size for a first coffee date (optional, labeled as estimate)

Example partner-facing frame:
> NCR university belt → ~A active students in AfterClass near your catchment → ~B venue selections / month across the network → founding Spots get priority placement while density is scarce.

Keep national 3.5M as a *small* expansion footnote, not the hero.

**What’s working**
- Honest “indicative pricing”
- Two-sided monetization without sounding extractive on day one

**Gaps**

- No partner ROI frame (what they pay vs what they get)

**Include next**

### Slide 9 — Why AfterClass Wins (Competition)
**Current:** Feature matrix vs Tinder/Bumble, Hinge/OkCupid, Campus Events

**What’s working**
- You don’t claim “no competition”
- Meet-first + café integration is a real wedge

**Gaps**
- Wrong comparison set for café decision-makers
- Feature matrices where you check every box are a known investor red flag — same risk here
- Ignores “do nothing” and real alternatives: FB groups, campus orgs, foodpanda promos, student ambassador flyers, TikTok, Grab deals

**Include next — dual competition story**
1. **For students (keep short):** vs chat-first dating apps  
2. **For cafés (primary):** vs other student acquisition channels

Partner 2×2 suggestion:

| | Low intent traffic | High intent traffic |
|--|--------------------|---------------------|
| **Unverified / noisy** | Promo blasts, walk-ins | Open dating apps nearby |
| **Verified / campus** | Campus orgs, flyers | **AfterClass Spots** |

Or “Why a café wins with us”:
- Students already decided to meet (intent)
- Venue is chosen in-product (distribution)
- Verified college-only (brand-safe)
- Daytime / coffee-first (fits café ops)

### Slide 10 — Founding Partner Offer ⚠️ Second-highest priority
**Current:** Guaranteed foot traffic · Featured discovery · Branding · Co-marketing · ₱0 · 3 months · Priority rates

**What’s working**
- Clear CTA; limited slots; low-friction trial

**Gaps**
- **“Guaranteed student foot traffic” is the riskiest line in the deck** unless you can contractually or historically back it
- Deliverables are vague (what is “featured”? how many impressions? which campuses?)

- No exclusivity / radius / category rules (important for cafés)

**Include next**
- Replace “Guaranteed” with evidence-backed language:  
  **“Priority placement in Spot discovery for students matching near you”**  
  + real selection / meetup counts if available
- Concrete founding package:
  - In-app Spot profile (photos, hours, tags)
  - Placement rules (distance, availability, ranking)
  - Physical kit (table tent, window sticker) — if real
  - Co-marketing (IG post, campus story, founder shout) — specify cadence
  - Reporting: monthly meetup selections + completed visits (if measurable)
- Terms table:

| Item | Founding term |
|------|----------------|
| Join fee | ₱0 |
| Trial | 3 months |
| Spots available | N in Metro Manila (scarcity) |
| After trial | Locked founding rate ₱X/mo |

## Missing Slides to Add (Partner-Optimized)

### A. Why Now (insert after Solution or before Market)
YC requires timing. For partners:

> Students already live on phones; trust in open dating apps is down; cafés need weekday daytime demand; Metro Manila’s campus density makes venue-led dating operationally possible *now*. AfterClass is live with 1,000+ verified students before Spot supply is built — partners who enter early own placement while inventory is scarce.

Categories to cite only if true: behavior shift (meet IRL), product readiness (live app), supply scarcity (few Spots vs growing demand).

### B. Team (insert before Ask / Close)
Even for partnerships, trust is personal.
- Founders + relevant proof (built consumer apps, campus distribution, hospitality relationships)
- “We operate the student side — you operate the venue. We bring demand; you host the date.”

### C. Partner Proof / Case Snapshot (highly recommended if any pilot exists)
One slide beats ten claims:
- Café name (or anonymized)
- Week 1–4: Spot views → selections → visits
- Quote from owner / barista
- Photo of students / Spot branding in situ (with permission)

If no café pilot yet, use **student meetup proof** as proxy and label honestly: “Demand exists; Spot network is what we’re building with founding partners.”

## Partner Q&A Prep (Expect These)

1. How many people will walk into *my* café in the first 30 days?
2. How do you choose which Spot to recommend?
3. Do competitors near me also get listed?
4. What happens after the free 3 months?
5. How do you verify students are real / still enrolled?
6. Who is liable if a date goes badly at my venue?
7. Can we see the in-app listing before we sign?
8. What creative / branding do we need to provide?
9. Which campuses actually drive traffic to my location?
10. What does success look like at day 90 — and what if it undershoots?

Draft answers into the deck’s proof slides so the conversation is evidence-led, not defensive.

## Stage Fit

This is closer to a **pre-seed / seed GTM partnership pitch** than a Series A investor deck.

- **Focus:** Problem clarity, live demand, partner offer, team trust  
- **Acceptable:** Incomplete café ROI if student demand proof is strong and terms are ₱0 risk  
- **Not acceptable:** Guarantees without data; vanity traction; dating-app feature bingo as the main “why us”

**Key partner question the deck must answer:**  
> “Is there real student demand near me, and is AfterClass the lowest-risk way to capture first-date visits?”
